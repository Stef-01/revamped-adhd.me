How ADHDme uses automated decision-making

This statement is published to meet the Privacy Act’s automated-decision-making transparency requirements, which commence on 10 December 2026, and is kept in step with the site itself. Last reviewed 16 September 2026.

What information is used

None about you. The pages are built ahead of time and served the same way to everybody. The only thing the site reads back from your browser is whether you have agreed to the privacy notice, and that value never leaves your device.

What is automated

  • The order of the GP list: roster order, the same for everybody, not ranked and not personalised.
  • Adding a source tag to a booking link when you follow it, so the practice’s own reporting can see that the visit came from ADHDme. The tag names the site and the page you pressed from, never you.

What is never automated

  • No clinical decision of any kind: no diagnosis, no triage, no assessment of symptoms.
  • No decision to deny care.
  • No inference about you.
  • No ordering of patients by need or by how unwell they are, and no list of who is most at risk.
  • No automated re-enabling of contact after you opt out. Opt-out is permanent.

Human controls

  • A person maintains the roster. A clinician can ask at any time for their listing to be corrected or removed, and a person makes that change.
  • A person reads every email sent to info@adhdme.au.
  • This statement is revised whenever the software’s behaviour changes, and the date above moves with it.
