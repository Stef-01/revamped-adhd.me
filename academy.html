How the course works

Written to be picked up after a gap. Each module opens with a short refresher, the terms it relies on, and the abbreviations spelled out the first time they appear.

Every module runs the same sequence. You answer an opening question before the teaching, work through the lessons with a knowledge check at the points that matter, apply the clinical tool to a case, then sit a ten-question test. Every question marks itself and explains the answer.

Scope

This academy does not replace jurisdiction-specific prescribing requirements, formal clinical guidelines, specialist consultation or individualised medical judgement. It is here to support them: sharper reasoning, rehearsed decisions, and the evidence close to hand. If anything here conflicts with your local requirements, the local requirements win. The decision always rests with you and the person in front of you.

What this course means by ADHD

A stable neurodevelopmental trait, expressed as a state that moves with context. The diagnosis describes the trait. Almost everything you can change lives in the modifiers.

Track 1

General practice

Assess, make it safe, titrate against targets. 10 minutes: 6 of learning, 4 of checks.

Before you start

  • Prescribing authority for ADHD medicines differs by state and territory. Check yours before acting.

Lessons

1Assess and triage2 min

A positive ASRS screen earns a full assessment. It is not a diagnosis. Establish three things: enough symptoms now, several present before age 12, impairment in two or more settings.

Time course separates the differentials. ADHD is lifelong and trait-like. Mood disorders come in episodes.

What to hold on to

  • ASRS Part A: four or more in the shaded range means assess further.
  • Ask about sleep and substances every time. Both amplify and both are treatable.
  • Refer: psychosis, bipolar disorder, active dependence, significant cardiac disease, pregnancy, diagnostic doubt.

Knowledge check

A patient describes two-week stretches of three hours’ sleep, feeling unstoppable and overspending, between ordinary months. Priority?

Answer C. Episodic elevated mood is assessed first. Stimulants can destabilise untreated bipolar disorder.

2Baseline and safe prescribing2 min

Stimulants raise heart rate and blood pressure a little on average. You only know who that matters for if you measured first.

Substance use is more common in untreated ADHD. Past misuse changes the choice of medicine and the monitoring, not the right to treatment.

What to hold on to

  • Before starting: blood pressure, heart rate, weight, cardiac history, family history of sudden death under 40. ECG only if flagged.
  • Misuse concern: long-acting formulation, real-time prescription monitoring, one prescriber, one pharmacy.
  • Never combine stimulants with monoamine oxidase inhibitors. Raise pregnancy plans at the first visit.

Knowledge check

Normal blood pressure, no symptoms, but his brother died suddenly at 36. Next step?

Answer A. Early sudden death in a first-degree relative is the flag that warrants cardiac assessment first.

3Titrate, review, hold continuity2 min

Agree on two or three problems the patient chose, rated 0 to 10, and titrate against those. Ask when the medicine starts working and when it wears off.

Fix the fixable before escalating: breakfast before the dose for appetite, earlier dosing for insomnia. If one stimulant class fails, try the other.

What to hold on to

  • Every review: blood pressure, heart rate, weight, target ratings. Yearly: still needed, still the right dose?
  • Not working: check adherence, timing, sleep and comorbidity before raising the dose.
  • Give the patient a one-page written plan. Plan interstate moves early; rules differ.

Knowledge check

On a long-acting stimulant taken at 10.30am, she cannot fall asleep before 1am. First step?

Answer C. Timing is the first lever. A late-morning dose can still be active at midnight.

Check yourself

Knowledge check

Which referral to an occupational therapist is most useful?

Answer D. A baseline and a target make the referral measurable for everyone.

Sources

  • Australian Evidence-Based Clinical Practice Guideline for ADHD (AADPA, 2022)
  • Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS-v1.1), World Health Organization

Track 2

Psychology

An assessment that holds up, and therapy built for ADHD. 10 minutes: 6 of learning, 4 of checks.

Before you start

  • Medication decisions belong to the prescriber. Your observations of response and side effects are useful to them.

Symbol explorer

The same symbols, out of the scene. Before you open one, try to recall what it stands for; then tap, hover or focus it to check.

Lessons

1Assessment that holds up2 min

Use a structured interview such as the DIVA-5 and get a concrete example for every criterion you endorse. Rating scales are sensitive, not specific: anxiety, depression and poor sleep all score high.

ADHD is developmental. Inattention that began in adulthood, after trauma or illness, needs another explanation first.

What to hold on to

  • Seek collateral. Missing childhood records lower certainty; they do not exclude the diagnosis. Document what you could not obtain.
  • Overlap is the rule. Formulate what each condition explains rather than picking one.
  • Lifelong and continuous points to ADHD. Episodic points to a mood disorder.

Knowledge check

A client scores very high on a self-report ADHD scale. What does that tell you?

Answer B. High scores also occur in anxiety, depression and sleep deprivation.

2Therapy built for ADHD, and the team2 min

Cognitive behavioural therapy designed for adult ADHD improves symptoms and functioning on top of medication. The active ingredient is structured skills practice: one calendar, one task list, first steps small enough to start now. Expect the system to collapse; restarting without self-criticism is the skill.

Emotional dysregulation is common in adult ADHD though absent from the criteria. “Rejection sensitive dysphoria” describes a real experience but is not a diagnosis.

What to hold on to

  • Every session ends with one small, specific practice task. Adapt the frame: visible agenda, written summary, reminders.
  • Shame drives avoidance. After a late diagnosis, make room for grief before skills.
  • Suicide risk is raised in ADHD. Ask directly, write a safety plan, tell the GP.

Knowledge check

A client keeps abandoning her planner after a bad week. Best response?

Answer C. Lapses are expected. A rehearsed restart matters more than a perfect streak.

Check yourself

Knowledge check

She says her medication makes her feel flat and asks whether to halve it.

Answer A. Pass the observation on. The dosing decision belongs with the prescriber.

Knowledge check

In passing, a client says “everyone would be better off without me”.

Answer D. A remark like this is a cue to ask directly, now.

Track 3

Occupational therapy

Start from what fails, then scaffold time, tasks and environment. 10 minutes: 6 of learning, 4 of checks.

Before you start

  • Trial evidence for occupational therapy in adult ADHD is small. Measure your own outcomes.

Symbol explorer

The same symbols, out of the scene. Before you open one, try to recall what it stands for; then tap, hover or focus it to check.

Lessons

1Assess from the occupation2 min

Start with the COPM: the person names up to five daily activities going worst and rates performance and satisfaction from 1 to 10. That is your baseline and your outcome.

Walk through a weekday hour by hour. Breakdowns cluster at transitions. Then name the executive demand that fails: starting, sequencing, estimating time, holding steps in mind, shifting or stopping.

What to hold on to

  • COPM: a change of two or more points is clinically meaningful. Re-score at eight to twelve weeks.
  • Find a routine that already works and copy its conditions.
  • Observe the task where you can. What people report and what they do differ.

Knowledge check

A client is always on time for football training but late for work. Most useful step?

Answer D. Success conditions transfer. That is why you map the day.

2Scaffold, adjust, hand on2 min

Make time visible with analogue clocks and visual timers where time is lost. Put the cue where the action happens, give each essential item one home, and remove steps before adding systems.

Use CO-OP: goal, plan, do, check. You ask the questions; the person generates the strategy, which is why it lasts.

What to hold on to

  • Sensory and workplace changes: trial one at a time and re-rate the goal. Evidence is thin; say so.
  • Reasonable adjustments are a legal duty under the Disability Discrimination Act 1992. JobAccess advises and may fund. Disclosure is the person’s choice.
  • Plan hard tasks inside the medication window. Refer mood, risk, sleep and eating concerns promptly.

Knowledge check

He keeps forgetting medication kept in a bathroom cabinet. Simplest effective change?

Answer B. A cue tied to an existing habit at the point of performance beats a reminder that can be dismissed.

Check yourself

Knowledge check

A client wants adjustments at work but fears telling her manager she has ADHD.

Answer C. Requests can be framed as needs. How much to disclose is hers to decide.

Knowledge check

On a home visit, a client says she has been thinking about ending her life.

Answer A. Act immediately, through your risk pathway and the GP.

Sources

  • Australian Evidence-Based Clinical Practice Guideline for ADHD (AADPA, 2022)
  • Canadian Occupational Performance Measure (COPM), 5th edition
  • JobAccess, Australian Government

Track 4

Exercise physiology

Safe on stimulants, honest about the effect, designed to last. 10 minutes: 6 of learning, 4 of checks.

Before you start

  • Exercise is an adjunct. It does not replace assessment or first-line treatment.

Symbol explorer

The same symbols, out of the scene. Before you open one, try to recall what it stands for; then tap, hover or focus it to check.

Lessons

1Safe on stimulants2 min

Run the Adult Pre-Exercise Screening System and add a full medicine and supplement list, including the time ADHD medication is taken. Record resting heart rate and blood pressure at baseline.

Stimulants raise heart rate, so age-predicted zones overstate effort. Prescribe by perceived exertion and the talk test. Appetite suppression means many clients arrive under-fuelled: ask when they last ate.

What to hold on to

  • Pre-workout caffeine adds to the stimulant’s effect on heart rate and blood pressure. Drop it.
  • Red flags: chest pain, fainting, sustained palpitations, unusual breathlessness. Stop and refer.
  • Report with the numbers and the time of the last dose. Do not advise on medication.

Knowledge check

A new client takes lisdexamfetamine and uses a caffeine pre-workout. First priority?

Answer B. Stimulant plus high-dose caffeine is additive. Removing it is the simplest safety step.

2Dose it, and design it to last2 min

Twenty to thirty minutes of moderate aerobic exercise gives a small to moderate lift in attention and executive function for about an hour. Place it directly before demanding mental work. Aerobic exercise has the best support; mind-body exercise shows a small effect, and coordinative exercise has too few studies to call.

Dropout is an executive problem, not a motivation problem.

What to hold on to

  • Design for adherence: short, varied, fixed time, a partner expecting them, immediate feedback, near-zero set-up.
  • Agree a minimum session and “never miss twice”. After a lapse: no comment, lower load, rebook on the spot.
  • Send the prescriber resting heart rate and blood pressure trends and any unintended weight loss.

Knowledge check

A student struggles most with afternoon study. When is exercise most useful?

Answer D. The effect lasts about an hour, so place it directly before the task.

Check yourself

Knowledge check

Mid-session, a client on a stimulant reports sudden palpitations and light-headedness.

Answer A. New cardiac symptoms on a stimulant need medical review first.

Knowledge check

He asks whether exercise can replace his medication.

Answer C. Be accurate about the size of the effect and keep medication with the prescriber.

Sources

  • Exercise and Sports Science Australia: Adult Pre-Exercise Screening System
  • Australian Evidence-Based Clinical Practice Guideline for ADHD (AADPA, 2022)
  • Module 06 of this course: acute exercise and cognition

Track 5

Nutrition

Appetite on stimulants, red flags, and the diet evidence stated plainly. 10 minutes: 6 of learning, 4 of checks.

Before you start

  • Medical nutrition therapy and eating disorders belong with an Accredited Practising Dietitian.

Symbol explorer

The same symbols, out of the scene. Before you open one, try to recall what it stands for; then tap, hover or focus it to check.

Lessons

1Appetite, weight and red flags2 min

Reduced appetite is the commonest stimulant side effect. Hunger vanishes through the middle of the day and returns hard in the evening. Plan around it: a substantial breakfast with protein before the dose, then eating by the clock, with alarms and small energy-dense food.

Keep food low-effort: five default meals and a saved grocery order outlast any detailed meal plan.

What to hold on to

  • Weigh regularly. Unintended loss of about five per cent in three months: tell the GP.
  • ADHD carries higher rates of binge eating and bulimia. SCOFF: two or more positive answers means refer.
  • Medication decisions are the prescriber’s. Help the client raise appetite at their next review.

Knowledge check

She asks whether to stop her ADHD medication because it ruins her appetite.

Answer D. Your part is the eating strategy and encouraging the conversation.

2The evidence, stated plainly2 min

Omega-3 shows a small effect at best, mostly in children, far below medication. Elimination diets have little adult evidence and real risks of inadequacy and disordered eating; they are not routine. Sugar has not been shown to cause or worsen ADHD, and links between eating pattern and ADHD are observational only. The priority is regular, varied, adequate meals.

Micronutrients: test where deficiency is plausible, then correct. Never supplement iron blind.

What to hold on to

  • Caffeine adds to stimulant effects on heart rate, anxiety and sleep. Ask how much and when.
  • Tell the GP every supplement the client takes. Patients often do not.
  • Useful wording: “The effect is small, and it would be in addition to your treatment, not instead of it.”

Knowledge check

She wants fish oil instead of starting medication.

Answer A. State the size of the effect and keep the medication decision with her doctor.

Check yourself

Knowledge check

A client answers yes to three SCOFF questions.

Answer C. A positive screen needs medical and specialist dietetic assessment.

Knowledge check

On a stimulant, she drinks four energy drinks a day and sleeps poorly.

Answer B. High caffeine with a stimulant is a likely, fixable cause of poor sleep.

Sources

  • Australian Evidence-Based Clinical Practice Guideline for ADHD (AADPA, 2022)
  • Dietitians Australia: find an Accredited Practising Dietitian
  • Morgan, Reid and Lacey, The SCOFF questionnaire, BMJ, 1999

Track 6

Naturopathy

Safe scope beside medical care, interactions, and the evidence stated plainly. 10 minutes: 6 of learning, 4 of checks.

Before you start

  • You work as an adjunct to medical care. Never advise stopping, reducing or delaying prescribed medication.

Lessons

1Scope and red flags2 min

Diagnosis and medication decisions belong to medical practitioners. A client who suspects ADHD needs assessment by a GP first, not supplements. A client who wants to change medication takes that to their prescriber, with your support.

Take a complete list at the first visit: prescriptions with dose and timing, over-the-counter products, herbs, energy drinks, pre-workout powders and recreational substances.

What to hold on to

  • Refer urgently: suicidal thoughts, chest pain, fainting or palpitations on a stimulant, signs of mania or psychosis.
  • Refer promptly: rapid weight loss, suspected eating disorder, pregnancy or planned pregnancy.
  • You cannot check interactions with a medicine you did not ask about.

Knowledge check

A client wants to stop her ADHD medication and “go natural”.

Answer D. Changes to prescribed medication are made with the prescriber.

2Interactions that matter2 min

Guarana, high-dose caffeine, bitter orange (synephrine) and yohimbe all add to a stimulant’s effect on heart rate and blood pressure. Ephedra is not legally available in Australia. Check “energy”, “fat-burner” and “pre-workout” blends, which often combine several.

St John’s wort speeds the breakdown of many medicines, including the contraceptive pill, and risks serotonin toxicity with antidepressants, which many adults with ADHD take.

What to hold on to

  • Liquorice in regular amounts can raise blood pressure.
  • Kava carries liver warnings. Sedating herbs add to alcohol and other sedatives.
  • Melatonin is a scheduled medicine in Australia. Lead with sleep routine, and ask a pharmacist when unsure.

Knowledge check

On a stimulant, an SSRI and the contraceptive pill, she asks about St John’s wort for mood.

Answer B. Two clinically important interactions, either one reason enough.

3The evidence, and the team2 min

Omega-3 shows a small effect at best. Correcting a deficiency confirmed by testing is sound care. Saffron, pine bark extract, bacopa and ginkgo rest on a few small, short trials, mostly in children: promising is not proven. Homeopathy has no reliable evidence for any condition (NHMRC, 2015), and detoxes and megavitamins have none in ADHD. These cost money and delay effective care.

Regular sleep, regular meals and daily movement have better support than any product.

What to hold on to

  • One or two changes at a time. Long protocols fail and add shame.
  • With consent, write to the GP: product, dose, start date.
  • Every recommendation gets a target and a review at eight to twelve weeks. No benefit: stop it.

Knowledge check

A parent asks about a homeopathic remedy for her adult son’s ADHD.

Answer C. Honesty about ineffective treatments protects the client’s money and time.

Check yourself

Knowledge check

A new client believes she has ADHD and asks for herbs to treat it.

Answer A. Assessment comes first. Treating an undiagnosed condition delays proper care.

Sources

  • Australian Evidence-Based Clinical Practice Guideline for ADHD (AADPA, 2022)
  • NHMRC, Evidence on the effectiveness of homeopathy for treating health conditions, 2015
  • Therapeutic Goods Administration: safety information on kava and St John’s wort

Module 01ADHD as a Dynamic State: Ecological Phenotyping

Replace the average patient with the within-person patient.

Use with guardrails75 min5 lessons

Deploy the methods. Automated phenotyping is still emerging.

If it has been a while

This module is about how one patient’s ADHD varies from day to day, rather than relying on one score at review. Keep three words apart as you read: trait (the stable diagnosis), state (how the person is functioning right now) and modifier (whatever shifts the state, such as sleep or the medication window). Nothing here replaces the diagnostic assessment; it sits after it.

ASRS
Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale
Trait
The enduring neurodevelopmental liability. Relatively stable across time and context.
State
How the person is functioning executively and emotionally right now.
Modifier
Any factor that plausibly shifts the state: sleep, urgency, interest, medication window, interruption, reproductive stage.
Ecological momentary assessment (EMA)
Brief repeated sampling of experience in real settings, rather than one retrospective recall at review.
Ecological phenotyping
Describing how a stable diagnosis is actually expressed across time, task, setting and treatment condition.

What you will be able to do

  • Separate trait-level ADHD from state-dependent amplification of impairment.
  • Build a within-person variability formulation from repeated brief measures.
  • Hold the line between temporal association and causation.
  • Turn a fortnight of observations into two or three testable hypotheses.

Before you start

A 32-year-old solicitor says her medication only works some days. The global symptom scale has barely moved. What is the best next step?

Answer B. A global score averages across contexts, so it can stay flat while function swings widely. Find the within-person pattern before assuming the dose is the problem.

Lessons

1The average-patient problem12 min

A global rating compresses many different contexts into a single number. That is useful for diagnosis and broad tracking. It also hides the question you actually need answered: when does this person function well, when do they fall apart, and what is different on those days?

Ecological phenotyping does not replace the diagnosis. It adds a second layer underneath it. The diagnosis describes a relatively stable developmental syndrome; the phenotype describes how that syndrome is expressed across time, task, setting and treatment condition.

So a patient can have entirely stable ADHD and still swing between near-normal function and serious executive impairment, depending on structure, reward, sleep, sensory load, mood or where they are in the medication window.

Pearl

  • Ask for the best and worst day of the last fortnight, then ask what was different. That single contrast often does more than the whole rating scale.
  • A stable ASRS (Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale) with a swinging function report is a within-person signal, not a measurement failure.

Knowledge check

A patient’s symptom scale is unchanged at review, but she describes two excellent weeks and one week she could barely work. What does the flat score most likely mean?

Answer C. Global scales compress context. A flat score with a volatile function report is exactly the case for a Variability Map.

2Trait, state, modifier12 min

Three words do most of the work in this module.

Trait

The enduring neurodevelopmental liability.

State

How the person is functioning right now, executively and emotionally.

Modifier

Anything that plausibly shifts the state.

Common modifiers
  • Short sleep
  • Acute stress
  • Emotional activation
  • Task ambiguity
  • Urgency
  • Interest
  • Interruptions
  • Medication offset
  • Physical activity
  • Reproductive stage
  • External structure

A bad afternoon following five hours of sleep, constant interruption and a medication that has worn off is not evidence that the medication has failed. The reverse holds too: excellent performance on a deadline does not disprove ADHD.

Pitfall

  • Calling a modifier a cause. Short sleep amplifies impairment; it does not create the trait.
  • Judging the trait on a deadline day. Urgency-dependent performance is a modifier effect and says nothing about whether ADHD is present.

Knowledge check

Which statement correctly separates trait from state?

Answer B. The trait clause stays true on a good day. That is the test. If a statement stops being true when the patient functions well, it is describing state.

3Sampling without burden14 min

Ecological momentary assessment in clinic should be brief and purposeful. You are not recreating a research protocol. You are sampling enough real-world moments to find patterns that retrospective recall blurs.

Seven to fourteen days. Prompts that take seconds, not nightly questionnaires. Capture the task, the executive state, the impairment, the relevant context, the medication timing. Expect gaps in the data, and resist treating every fluctuation as disease. What you are looking for is a relationship that repeats often enough to be worth a hypothesis.

Numbers that matter

  • 7–14 days, 2–4 prompts a day, under 30 seconds each. Beyond that, completion rates fall and the data thins out where you need it most.
  • Aim for 60% completion or better. A patchy record is still usable if the gaps are not systematic (for example, always missing the bad afternoons).

Knowledge check

Which sampling plan best balances signal against burden for a working adult?

Answer B. Brief, repeated, contextual prompts over one to two weeks find patterns that recall blurs, without the drop-off that long nightly forms produce.

4Pattern is not causation12 min

If short-sleep nights are repeatedly followed by worse task initiation, the defensible sentence is: impairment appears higher after short sleep, so sleep is a plausible modifier worth testing. The indefensible one is: sleep deprivation causes this patient’s ADHD.

Useful hypotheses are specific and falsifiable. Move unstructured admin into the 9am–noon window and task-start latency should improve relative to late afternoon. That can be wrong, which is what makes it worth testing.

Resist generating dozens of automated correlations. More associations buy you false precision, and false precision makes decisions worse.

Pearl

  • Write hypotheses as predictions with a direction and a window: moving admin to 9–12 should cut start latency versus 3–5pm. If it cannot be wrong, it is not a hypothesis.
  • Cap the list at two or three. More associations mean more false precision, not more insight.

Knowledge check

Short-sleep nights are repeatedly followed by worse task initiation. What may you conclude?

Answer C. A repeated association earns a hypothesis, not a cause. It is specific enough to test, which is exactly what makes it useful.

5From observation to intervention10 min

At review, four questions:

  • What repeatedly predicts good function?
  • What repeatedly predicts breakdown?
  • What can be modified now?
  • What still needs testing rather than assuming?

The answers might point to environmental redesign, sleep work, behavioural support, altered medication coverage, or some combination. What you are after is not more data. It is a better next decision.

Pitfall

  • Changing two levers at once. Shift the medication and the schedule in the same fortnight and you cannot attribute the result to either.
  • Collecting a second fortnight before acting on the first. The point of the map is a better next decision, not a bigger dataset.

Knowledge check

The map shows breakdown after short sleep and during unstructured review. The patient wants to try a higher dose and a new morning routine this week. Best advice?

Answer D. One variable at a time preserves your ability to attribute the change. The map already justifies acting; it does not justify acting twice at once.

Case laboratory

The solicitor’s one-week record shows:

  • Good executive performance 9am to noon
  • Deterioration after nights below six hours
  • Severe impairment during unstructured document review
  • Strong function in client-facing work
  • Irritability near medication offset
  • Little impairment on highly urgent deadlines

Case question

Given the one-week record, which formulation is best supported?

Answer B. Strong client-facing and deadline performance alongside collapse in unstructured work is a fit problem plus a coverage question, not evidence of an inadequate dose.

Case question

Which observation from this record must NOT be called causal?

Answer D. Every line in the record is an observed co-occurrence over one week in one person. Each is worth testing; none is established as a cause.

Debrief.

The record does not justify automatic dose escalation. A defensible formulation is stable ADHD with strong reward and structure sensitivity, sleep-related amplification, and a possible late-day coverage issue.

Trial protected admin work in the strongest cognitive window. Address the repeated short sleep. Test whether the offset timing replicates before changing anything about the medication.

Key points

  • A stable diagnosis and a highly variable presentation are not in conflict. Trait describes the liability; state describes today.
  • Global scales average across contexts. That is exactly what hides the information you need to act on.
  • Seven to fourteen days of brief sampling beats one long retrospective conversation.
  • A repeated association earns a hypothesis, never a cause.
  • Finish with two or three hypotheses and one changed lever, not a larger dataset.

1From dose escalation to response surfaces12 min

Module 05AuDHD: Transdiagnostic Neurodevelopmental Care

Two labels do not make a formulation; find the domains that are interacting.

Use with guardrails75 min5 lessons

Domain-based formulation is deployable now. Mechanistic precision remains emerging.

If it has been a while

AuDHD is the informal term for co-occurring ADHD and autism. Rather than stacking two labels, the module works at the level of functional domains: activation, attention, switching, sensory regulation, social function, emotion, structure and interests. The recurring point is that the same behaviour can come from different mechanisms, and a treatment that helps one domain can expose difficulty in another.

Domain-level formulation
Analysing interacting functional domains rather than stacking diagnostic labels.
Transition cost
The effort required to change cognitive set, distinct from impulsive stimulus-driven switching.

What you will be able to do

  • Formulate co-occurring ADHD and autism at the level of functional domains.
  • Distinguish superficially similar complaints arising from different mechanisms.
  • Recognise when an intervention improves one domain while exposing difficulty in another.
  • Build interventions around activation, attention, switching, sensory regulation, social function, emotion, structure and interests.

Lessons

1From lifestyle advice to state intervention8 min

Traditional advice treats exercise as a long-horizon health behaviour. There is a different possibility worth testing:

  • Movement
  • Acute physiological and cognitive state change
  • High-demand task

Candidate windows: before prolonged administration, before study, before complex meetings, before response-inhibition demands, or ahead of a predictable afternoon decline.

Pearl

  • Place the bout 20–60 minutes before the target task. That is the window in which acute effects on inhibitory control have been observed.
  • Pick the target task first (the 2pm document review), then the bout. Not the other way round.

Knowledge check

What is the key difference between exercise as lifestyle advice and exercise as a state intervention?

Answer C. The reframe is about timing and measurement: a bout placed ahead of a defined cognitive demand, judged on that demand.

2What the evidence can and cannot say10 min

A 2026 adult ADHD systematic review and meta-analysis reports a moderate acute improvement in inhibitory control. The chronic-exercise evidence is less consistent across outcomes.

That is interesting enough to test safely in suitable patients. It is not enough to guarantee benefit for any individual, not enough to prescribe one universal duration, intensity or modality, and nowhere near enough to replace pharmacological or psychological treatment.

Numbers that matter

  • The adult meta-analytic signal is a moderate acute improvement in inhibitory control. Chronic-exercise findings are less consistent across outcomes.
  • Typical acute protocols in the literature: 20–30 minutes of moderate aerobic work, or roughly 16 minutes of interval training. Treat these as starting points to test, not prescriptions.

Knowledge check

Answer B. A group-level average does not predict an individual. It justifies a trial, not a promise.

Module 07Strengths-Based Care: and Environmental Precision

Find the conditions where this person works well, then build more of them, without pretending the impairment is gone.

Use with guardrails75 min5 lessons

Strengths identification can be used now. Claims that ADHD inherently confers specific strengths should be avoided.

If it has been a while

Strengths-based care in this module means finding the conditions under which a patient works well and building more of them, without claiming that ADHD confers special abilities. You will be asked to describe an environment in terms of its reward, structure, interruptions, autonomy and sensory load. Every strength is treated as context-dependent and as carrying a trade-off.

Person-environment fit
The match between an individual’s functional profile and the reward, structure, interruption, autonomy and sensory characteristics of their setting.

What you will be able to do

  • Distinguish evidence-informed strengths-based care from superpower messaging.
  • Identify relative strengths without suggestion bias.
  • Analyse the reward, structure, interruption, autonomy and sensory architecture of an environment.
  • Evaluate strengths as context-dependent capacities that carry trade-offs.

Before you start

A patient has high symptom scores in two jobs. In Job A they are failing; in Job B they are thriving. Is symptom severity alone sufficient to explain function?

Answer B. Same person, same diagnosis, same scores, different function. The variable that moved was the environment.

1Strengths-based is not romanticisation10 min

The rule is simple enough to hold onto: observe strengths in the individual. Do not assign them by diagnosis.

  • A strength counts when other people independently seek it out.

2Ask neutral strengths questions10 min

Avoid: “What are your ADHD superpowers?” The question supplies its own answer.

Prefer:

  • What tasks reliably produce unusually good performance?
  • What do other people seek your help for?
  • When do you sustain attention without external coercion?
  • What kinds of problems do you solve better than peers?
  • Under what conditions do you become highly persistent?
  • Which environments have historically produced your best work?
  • Which apparent strength becomes harmful when overused?

  • What are your ADHD superpowers? supplies its own answer and flatters rather than informs.

3Analyse environmental architecture18 min

  • Feedback frequency
  • Autonomy
  • Novelty
  • Structure
  • Interruption density
  • Switching demand
  • Sensory load

  • Cognitive complexity
  • Time of day

  • Score the current role on the twelve dimensions from 0 to 2. Any role scoring high on distant reward, ambiguity, interruption density and low feedback predicts impairment regardless of dose.
  • The same diagnosis produces very different function in two roles. Fit is a variable, not a fixed property of the person.

  • Accountability

  • Complex work in high-function windows

Know what each allied-health discipline can do for adult ADHD, when to refer, and what you can run yourself in the meantime.

Pathway A

Holistic & lifestyle

Sleep, nutrition, mind-body practice and substances as modifiers you can measure. Not alternatives to treatment.

Refer when

  • Insomnia Severity Index ≥15 that persists after stimulant timing has been optimised: refer for CBT-I (cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia).
  • Suspected delayed sleep phase, sleep apnoea or restless legs: sleep medicine, not more sleep hygiene.
  • Disordered eating or restriction on stimulants: dietitian with eating-disorder experience, early.
  • Patient wants mindfulness or yoga: fine as an adjunct with a named target; not as a replacement for first-line treatment.

Lessons

1Sleep is a modifier you can move12 min

Short or irregular sleep is the most common amplifier of ADHD impairment and the one most often left unmeasured. Adults with ADHD carry high rates of insomnia and delayed sleep phase, and stimulant timing can add to both.

Before anything else, get three numbers: usual bedtime, usual wake time, and time of last stimulant dose. If the last dose lands within the drug’s coverage window of intended sleep, timing is the first lever.

A systematic review of CBT-I in neurodevelopmental conditions found short-term improvement in insomnia severity across two randomised trials and six before-after studies, mostly not maintained at follow-up, with moderate study quality. That is good enough to refer; not good enough to promise.

Numbers that matter

  • Insomnia Severity Index: 0–7 none, 8–14 subthreshold, 15–21 moderate, 22–28 severe. Refer at 15 or above after timing is fixed.
  • Two-week sleep diary before referral. CBT-I providers will need it anyway, and it often solves the case.

Knowledge check

A patient on lisdexamfetamine at 11am cannot fall asleep before 2am. ISI (Insomnia Severity Index) is 18. Best first step?

Answer B. A dose at 11am can still be active at midnight. Fix timing, measure, then refer with data.

2Nutrition, appetite and the stimulant10 min

Appetite suppression is expected; weight loss beyond 5% of body weight in three months is not benign. Weigh at every dose change and record it.

Front-load protein before the first dose and schedule an evening meal after offset. Skipped lunches are a coverage problem as much as a nutrition problem.

Omega-3 supplementation has been studied mostly in children. A 2023 meta-analysis of randomised trials found no improvement in core symptoms overall, with a possible signal for longer supplementation. It is safe, cheap and not a treatment for ADHD.

Pitfall

  • Calling omega-3, elimination diets or sugar restriction “evidence-based treatment” for adult ADHD. None meets that bar.
  • Missing restriction hiding behind stimulant appetite loss. Ask about eating history before prescribing, and again at every weight check.

Knowledge check

How should omega-3 supplementation be positioned for an adult with ADHD who asks about it?

Answer D. Honest positioning: safe, cheap, not a treatment. Long-duration signals are hypotheses, not findings.

3Mind-body practice as adjunct12 min

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis of mindfulness-based interventions in adults with ADHD found reductions in self-reported symptoms versus inactive controls, but no clear advantage over active psychological comparators. A 2023 meta-analysis of meditation-based mind-body interventions (mindfulness, yoga, tai chi) reported small effects on inattention, hyperactivity-impulsivity and executive function.

Small, real, and no better than other structured programmes. That supports offering mindfulness to patients who want it, with a defined target such as emotional reactivity or task re-entry after interruption, and a review date.

Pearl

  • Set one target and one measure before the programme: for example, self-rated emotional reactivity 0–4 at the same time daily, reviewed at eight weeks.
  • Eight-week programmes with daily home practice are what the trials used. Shorter or app-only formats are less studied.

Knowledge check

What does the adult meta-analytic evidence support saying about mindfulness for ADHD?

Answer A. Small effects, real but not superior to active comparators. Offer it with a target and a review date.

4Substances and the daily rhythm10 min

Caffeine, nicotine, alcohol and cannabis all shift the state you are trying to read. Ask about quantity and timing, not just use.

Late caffeine lengthens sleep onset; alcohol fragments the second half of the night; cannabis in the evening is often self-treatment for sleep or restlessness that a better regimen would remove.

Put these on the Variability Map from Module 01 as modifiers. The pattern usually shows within a fortnight.

Pearl

  • Caffeine half-life is roughly 5–6 hours; a 3pm double espresso is still half present at 9pm. Ask for a caffeine cut-off eight hours before intended sleep.
  • Ask about cannabis timing and purpose without judgement. Evening use for sleep is a treatable sleep problem.

Knowledge check

A patient reports evening cannabis “to switch off” and 2am sleep onset. What is the most useful framing?

Answer C. Evening cannabis for sleep is a symptom of an untreated sleep problem. Map it, address sleep, and reassess.

Pathway B

Speech pathology

Pragmatic language and discourse difficulties persist into adulthood and cost jobs and relationships. They are assessable and treatable.

Refer when

  • Repeated interview or meeting failure despite adequate symptom control.
  • Complaints of talking over people, losing the thread, or long disorganised explanations that others cannot follow.
  • Written communication that costs disproportionate time or triggers workplace complaints.
  • Any suspicion of co-occurring developmental language disorder or literacy difficulty that was never assessed.

Lessons

1Pragmatics is not a childhood problem12 min

Children with ADHD show higher rates of pragmatic difficulty than typically developing peers, with specific problems in inappropriate initiation, presupposition, social discourse and narrative coherence. These difficulties are greater than structural language alone explains.

A 2026 systematic review of discourse skills across the lifespan argues that adult presentations differ from childhood ones and that adult data have been largely ignored in past reviews. Clinically, that means asking rather than assuming.

In adults the same processes show up as interrupting, tangential answers, over-long narratives, missing the implied request in an email, and misreading sarcasm or tone.

Pearl

  • Discourse skills are exercised in every consultation: giving a history, describing a week, negotiating a plan. Watch how the patient organises a narrative before you decide whether to ask about communication.
  • Pragmatic difficulty in ADHD sits between typical development and autism in degree. Do not read it as autism by default.

Knowledge check

Which pattern in an adult most suggests a pragmatic language difficulty worth assessing?

Answer C. Initiation, coherence and presupposition are the domains implicated in ADHD. Loss of thread, interruption and missed implication map onto them directly.

2Screen in four minutes10 min

Ask the patient to describe their last week at work in two minutes. Note: does the narrative have a beginning, a point and an end? How many self-corrections? Do they check whether you are following?

Then ask: what do people say about how you communicate? Third-party feedback is more reliable than self-rating here.

Two or more of: incoherent narrative, frequent interruption, no listener-checking, repeated workplace feedback about communication. That is enough to refer.

Pearl

  • Record the two-minute narrative task at baseline and repeat it after intervention. It is a cheap, repeatable within-person measure.
  • Ask specifically about written communication. Email and chat expose planning and presupposition problems that speech can hide.

Knowledge check

What is the most informative single question when screening for pragmatic difficulty?

Answer A. Third-party feedback surfaces what the patient cannot see. The other questions rely on self-perception, which is exactly what is impaired.

3What the speech pathologist will do12 min

Expect formal assessment of discourse and pragmatics, plus screening of structural language and literacy, because co-occurring language disorder is common and often missed in ADHD.

Intervention is typically goal-based: turn-taking and listener-checking strategies, narrative scaffolds (situation, problem, action, result), explicit rules for written requests, and rehearsal of high-stakes contexts such as interviews or difficult conversations.

Ask for a report that names the goals, the baseline and the measure. A referral that says communication difficulties gets a general programme; one that says loses the thread in meetings, interrupts, misreads written requests gets a targeted one.

Pitfall

  • Assuming adult speech pathology is only for swallowing, voice or stroke. Adult pragmatic and cognitive-communication work is a core scope.
  • Sending the referral without the stimulant status. Medication changes communication measurably; the pathologist needs to know the treatment condition at assessment.

Knowledge check

Which referral wording is most likely to produce a useful speech pathology programme?

Answer D. Specific behaviours, treatment condition and a request for measurable goals produce a targeted programme.

4Communication as coverage10 min

Communication is one of the four titration axes in practice: breadth. Ask whether conversational control, listening and written output changed with the medication, and when in the day they fall apart.

A patient whose meetings go badly after 3pm has a coverage question and a communication question at once. Move the meeting or extend the coverage, then measure.

Share the two-minute narrative measure with the speech pathologist so both of you are tracking the same thing across treatment conditions.

Pearl

  • Add one line to every titration review: how did conversations and emails go this fortnight, and at what time of day were they worst?
  • Late-day communication failure is often offset, not personality.

Knowledge check

Meetings after 3pm go badly; morning meetings are fine. Stimulant taken 7am. First hypothesis to test?

Answer B. A time-locked pattern with a morning dose is a coverage hypothesis first. Test it by moving the meeting or adjusting coverage and measuring.

Pathway C

Occupational therapy

Routines, environments, time and sensory load: the executive scaffolding of daily life, built with the patient rather than for them.

Refer when

  • Impairment concentrated in daily-life execution: mornings, bills, housework, appointments, work organisation, despite adequate symptom control.
  • Sensory load (noise, light, open-plan offices) is a repeated modifier on the Variability Map.
  • Return-to-work, role change or parenting transition that has overwhelmed compensation.
  • Patient wants strategies rather than, or in addition to, medication, and coaching is unavailable or has not worked.

Lessons

1Occupation is the outcome that matters10 min

Occupational therapy treats performance of the activities a person needs and wants to do. For adult ADHD that means the morning routine, the inbox, the kitchen, the commute, the job.

The outcome measure is usually the patient’s own rating of performance and satisfaction on self-selected goals (the Canadian Occupational Performance Measure), scored 1–10 before and after. A change of two points is considered clinically meaningful.

This is the same logic as the functional targets in Module 02, applied to daily life instead of a treatment condition.

Numbers that matter

  • COPM (Canadian Occupational Performance Measure): performance and satisfaction each 1–10 on up to five patient-chosen goals. A two-point change is the conventional threshold for meaningful improvement.
  • Ask the patient to pick the goals. Therapist-chosen goals predict dropout.

Knowledge check

What is the primary outcome most OT (occupational therapy) programmes for adult ADHD are built around?

Answer B. OT outcomes are occupational: performance and satisfaction on goals the patient chose.

2What the trials show12 min

A randomised controlled trial of Work-MAP, an 11-session telehealth metacognitive intervention for adults with ADHD (n = 46), reported moderate-to-strong improvements in performance and satisfaction on work goals, executive function ratings and quality of life, maintained at three months.

A seven-session functional OT intervention for women with ADHD (n = 25, randomised) reduced self-reported symptoms and stress and improved daily performance at one week post-intervention, with no longer follow-up.

Small trials, self-report outcomes, short follow-up, no blinding. Enough to refer with confidence that the approach is coherent and acceptable; not enough to promise durable change.

Pitfall

  • Quoting effect sizes from a 46-person unblinded trial as if they were established. Say “small trials, promising, short follow-up”.
  • Expecting OT to change symptom scores. It targets function, and that is what it measures.

Knowledge check

How should the OT trial evidence for adult ADHD be summarised to a patient?

Answer D. Honest summary: coherent, acceptable, promising, small and short.

3Metacognitive strategy, not tips12 min

Effective OT for executive dysfunction teaches a general strategy loop (goal, plan, do, check) and coaches the patient to generate their own task-specific strategies. Handing over a list of tips is not the intervention.

Routine building: anchor new behaviours to existing fixed points (after the first coffee; when the car engine stops). Externalise time: visible timers, calendar blocks with buffers, a single capture point for tasks.

Sensory regulation: noise-cancelling in open-plan offices, a low-stimulus corner for hard tasks, movement breaks on a schedule rather than on impulse. These reduce the load the medication has to overcome.

Pearl

  • Ask for one anchored routine per fortnight. Three new routines at once fail by week two.
  • The best environmental change is the one the patient can make on Monday without buying anything.

Knowledge check

What distinguishes a metacognitive OT approach from generic organisational advice?

Answer A. Metacognitive strategy training builds a transferable process. Tips do not transfer.

4Referral and co-management11 min

Refer with the Variability Map. The OT can see which contexts and modifiers predict breakdown and target them directly.

Agree one shared measure: the COPM goals, re-rated at every prescriber review as well as in therapy. When the medication changes, the OT should know; when the routine changes, so should you.

Group and telehealth formats have been trialled and are acceptable to many adults with ADHD. Cost and waiting lists differ; ask what the local service actually offers before promising a format.

Pitfall

  • Referring “for executive function”. Refer for mornings, or bills, or the inbox. The OT will find the executive function.
  • Changing the dose in the same fortnight the OT starts a new routine. Two levers, no attribution.

Knowledge check

Which shared measure best supports prescriber-OT co-management?

Answer C. A single functional measure, rated in both settings, keeps the team working from one baseline.

Pathway D

Exercise physiology

Module 06 tested exercise as an acute state lever. This pathway is about who prescribes the programme, how it is made safe on stimulants, and how it is designed so an adult with ADHD actually keeps doing it.

Refer when

  • Cardiovascular risk factors, resting BP (blood pressure) ≥140/90 or resting pulse ≥100 on a stimulant, and the patient wants to exercise: EP-supervised programme after medical review.
  • Repeated failed attempts at self-directed exercise. Adherence design is the EP’s core skill.
  • Comorbid obesity, type 2 diabetes, musculoskeletal injury or chronic pain.
  • Patient wants a structured programme with progression and measurement rather than generic advice.

Lessons

1What chronic exercise can and cannot claim12 min

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis in adults with ADHD reported moderate acute improvement in inhibitory control, with less consistent chronic-exercise effects across executive and symptom outcomes. A 2025 meta-analysis on inhibitory control reached a similar conclusion.

Most chronic-programme trials are small, short (8–12 weeks) and unblinded, and paediatric findings do not transfer automatically to adults.

The defensible claim: exercise is a plausible adjunct with acute cognitive effects, general health benefit, and modest, uncertain chronic effects on ADHD outcomes. It is not a replacement for first-line treatment.

Numbers that matter

  • Typical trial programmes: 2–3 sessions a week, 30–60 minutes, moderate intensity, 8–12 weeks. Combined aerobic, strength and coordination programmes have been used in adult ADHD trials.
  • Cognitively engaging modalities (ball sports, martial arts, dance) show larger executive-function effects than pure aerobic work in paediatric meta-analyses. In adults this is a hypothesis.

Knowledge check

What is the accurate summary of chronic exercise evidence for adult ADHD?

Answer C. Acute: moderate and reproducible. Chronic: promising, small, short, inconsistent.

2Safety on stimulants12 min

Stimulants raise resting heart rate by roughly 3–10 beats per minute and systolic BP by roughly 2–5 mmHg on average, with wide individual variation. Exercise adds to both acutely.

Before recommending moderate-to-vigorous exercise: resting BP and pulse on the current dose, cardiovascular and family history, and a screen for exertional symptoms. Abnormal findings or symptoms need medical review before an EP (exercise physiologist) programme.

Hydration and heat matter more on stimulants. Appetite suppression plus training load plus reduced thirst cue is a recipe for under-fuelling.

Pitfall

  • Waving through high-intensity interval work in a patient whose resting pulse on stimulant is already 100.
  • Forgetting under-fuelling. Ask what they ate before training and whether weight is stable.

Knowledge check

A patient on dexamfetamine has resting BP 146/92 and pulse 102 and wants to start interval training. Best step?

Answer A. Abnormal resting values on a stimulant need medical review first. The programme can follow, supervised.

3Prescription the patient will keep12 min

Adherence, not modality, is the problem. Exercise physiologists design for it: fixed session slots anchored to existing routines, short sessions at first, immediate feedback (heart rate, reps, times), and progression the patient can see.

Interest and novelty are modifiers here as everywhere in ADHD. A programme that varies within a fixed structure outperforms a perfect programme the patient abandons in week three.

Ask the EP for a programme card with FITT (frequency, intensity, time and type) (frequency, intensity, time, type), a progression rule, and a fallback for missed sessions. The fallback is what saves the programme.

Pearl

  • Start at 2 sessions of 20 minutes anchored to an existing habit. Add duration before intensity.
  • A missed session rule: if I miss, I do ten minutes that day and the plan continues. Without it, one miss becomes a stop.

Knowledge check

Which programme design feature most predicts adherence in adults with ADHD?

Answer D. Design for the executive profile: anchors, feedback, novelty within structure, and a rule that survives a miss.

4Timing, measurement and the team9 min

Where the patient wants an acute cognitive effect, position sessions 20–60 minutes before the target task, as in Module 06, and measure that task.

Share two numbers with the EP: resting pulse and BP on the current dose, and any medication changes. Share two numbers back: sessions completed and the functional target.

Reassess at 12 weeks: adherence, resting cardiovascular measures, weight, and the functional target. Then decide whether the programme changes or the medication does. Not both at once.

Pearl

  • Sessions completed over sessions planned is the first outcome. Below 60% at six weeks, redesign before judging effect.
  • Weigh at 12 weeks. Training plus stimulant appetite loss can produce unplanned weight loss.

Knowledge check

At 12-week review, adherence is 45% and there is no change in the functional target. What follows?

Answer B. An effect cannot be judged on a programme that was not done. Adherence first.

Glossary

The working vocabulary of the course, with the module where each term is introduced.

Trait Module 01
The enduring neurodevelopmental liability. Relatively stable across time and context.
State Module 01
How the person is functioning executively and emotionally right now.
Modifier Module 01
Any factor that plausibly shifts the state: sleep, urgency, interest, medication window, interruption, reproductive stage.
Ecological momentary assessment (EMA) Module 01
Brief repeated sampling of experience in real settings, rather than one retrospective recall at review.
Ecological phenotyping Module 01
Describing how a stable diagnosis is actually expressed across time, task, setting and treatment condition.
Magnitude Module 02
How much a predefined functional target improved under a treatment condition.
Coverage Module 02
The temporal profile of benefit: when it begins, peaks and ends.
Breadth Module 02
Which functional domains improved, stayed the same, or worsened.
Cost Module 02
Sleep, appetite, cardiovascular measures, anxiety, emotional range, headache, rebound and quality of life.
Net utility Module 02
The overall balance of magnitude, coverage and breadth against cost. This is the basis for choosing a dose.
N-of-1-inspired titration Module 02
Borrowing the logic of sequential within-person comparison without claiming the randomised, blinded method.
Compensation load Module 03
The effort, scaffolding, distress and recovery cost required to sustain apparently normal performance.
Camouflaging Module 03
Self-monitoring and masking that make clinically significant difficulty invisible to observers. A formulation concept, not a criterion.
Transition vulnerability Module 03
The exposure of longstanding difficulty when a life change removes the scaffolding that was containing it.
Ascertainment bias Module 04
Systematic under-recognition arising from who gets referred and assessed, rather than from true differences in prevalence.
Prospective tracking Module 04
Recording forward in time to test whether a pattern replicates, instead of reconstructing it from memory.
Measurement invariance Module 09
Whether an instrument functions equivalently across groups. Partial invariance means scores are not directly interchangeable.
Domain-level formulation Module 05
Analysing interacting functional domains rather than stacking diagnostic labels.
Transition cost Module 05
The effort required to change cognitive set, distinct from impulsive stimulus-driven switching.
Person-environment fit Module 07
The match between an individual’s functional profile and the reward, structure, interruption, autonomy and sensory characteristics of their setting.
Minority stress Module 08
The cumulative burden of stigma, discrimination and vigilance, distinct from, and interacting with, baseline impairment.
Cultural safety Module 09
Care in which the patient can disclose fully. An input to diagnostic accuracy, not a courtesy applied afterwards.
Digital phenotyping Module 10
Using sleep, movement, physiological and self-report streams to characterise real-world function over time.
Validation boundary Module 10
The line between measurements adequate for clinical decisions and exploratory signals that are not.

Clinical tool status

Every ADHDme tool in this workbook is a structured clinical formulation aid. None is a validated psychometric instrument, and none becomes one until prospective validation has been completed. Use them to organise reasoning, make uncertainty visible and generate testable hypotheses. Do not use them in place of diagnostic criteria, validated monitoring, clinical guidelines or professional judgement.
