How handoffs are counted

The last thing this site can honestly observe is somebody leaving for a practice’s booking page. This page says how that is counted, where each count can be read, what you can switch off, and what cannot be seen from here.

The channels

  • The practice’s own reporting. Live. Every booking link carries a source tail (utm_source=adhd-me, utm_medium=referral, utm_campaign=<surface>, utm_content=<clinician>) into Healthengine, Halaxy, or the clinic’s own booking page, so the practice can see referrals from their side. Read it in the practice’s own reporting, source "adhd-me".
  • PostHog. Live. The product’s own analytics: the declared events below are sent there, and each browser becomes one row in a list of people — a random label such as "Visitor 3f9a21", when it first arrived, what brought it, how many clinician pages it has read and how many booking links it has followed. No name, no email, no address, nothing typed into anything. Session replay is on: what happens on the screen is recorded and can be played back as a silent video, with every field you could type into blanked before the recording is made. Opting out below stops it with everything else.
  • Google Analytics. The aggregate count, optional and separate. When a measurement ID is set, the same declared events go there too, without cookies and with advertising signals disabled.
  • This device’s tally. Live, local, and on whether or not anything above is configured. Each handoff is appended to a small log in this browser’s own storage: clinician, discipline, practice, surface and day. It never leaves the device.
  • How long the practice’s page held you. Live. A booking link opens in a new tab, so this page stays open behind it and can time how long you are gone before you come back — in bands, not to the second. Under thirty seconds is a glance; minutes is a form being filled in. It is the closest honest proxy for a booking, and it is a proxy.
  • Whether a booking followed. Cannot be observed from here. Neither Healthengine nor Halaxy nor a clinic’s booking form has a conversion endpoint for a third party. Nothing on this site claims a booking it cannot see.

Counting me, or not

One button, and it takes effect on this device straight away. Opting out stops every channel above except the practice’s own reporting, which is the practice’s to run. If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, this site treats that as already opted out and the button cannot override it.

The same switch works from a link: ?analytics=off on any page of this site opts this browser out, ?analytics=on puts it back.

The events this site declares

  • page-viewed: a page of this site was opened, named in the site’s own words.
  • landing-viewed: somebody arrived at the front door.
  • landing-cta: which named control was pressed — the header’s four, and the landing page’s two doors in the hero, the panels and the closing banner.
  • deck-viewed: somebody opened The Network, with how many practitioner cards the deck held.
  • deck-card-opened: which card on The Network was pressed, and that clinician’s discipline.
  • profile-viewed: somebody opened one clinician’s page — who, their discipline, their practice, and which surface they came from.
  • profile-engaged: one summary of how a clinician’s page was read, sent when the page is closed — how far down it was reached in four steps (the top, the fee table, the rest of the network, the foot of the page), roughly how many seconds it held the screen, and whether the reading ended in a booking link being followed. One per reading, not one per scroll.
  • booking-outbound: somebody left for a booking page — who they were booking with, their discipline, their practice, where the link lands, which surface it started on, which named link was pressed, and whether the link lands on a live diary or an enquiry form.
  • booking-returned: somebody came back to this tab after a booking page — who it was for, and roughly how long they were away. The absence of this event is the signal worth having: a handoff nobody comes back from is somebody who stayed.

Three things ride along with the events above. What the clinician is listed for — their discipline, the areas they work in, the age groups they see — which describes the practitioner rather than the reader, and is what lets us see which kinds of help the network is asked for and has too few people for. The channel a visit came from, taken from the tag on the link or, failing that, from the address of the site that sent you. And, when you arrive from one of our own posts, the subject of that post and the post itself, kept for the length of the visit so we can see which things we publish actually help somebody find a clinician.

Every event is checked against this list before it is sent, and so is every property value: the clinician, discipline and practice vocabularies are generated from the site’s own list of clinicians. An event outside the list, or a value outside its vocabulary, is refused and logged as analytics-refused rather than becoming a row that looks real. Add ?debug=analytics to any page to watch both in the console.

How to read the funnel

Landing → deck is whether one screen and one button are enough to make somebody look at the clinicians. Deck → profile is whether the cards say enough about a person to be worth opening. Profile → handoff is whether the page answers what somebody needs before they act. None is a conversion rate: the last step is a handoff, and whether an appointment happened is not observable from here. Somebody who arrives, reads the one sentence and decides this is not for them has been served, not lost.

What is kept, for how long, and how to ask for it to be deleted is in the privacy policy.
