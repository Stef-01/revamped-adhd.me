## The three, as they will run

**01. Trauma and ADHD look alike from the outside.**
Difficulty concentrating, restlessness and broken sleep appear in both, so the surface
presentation overlaps. What usually separates them is developmental history: ADHD traits
tend to show up early and across settings, while trauma responses more often track a
particular onset. They also co-occur often enough that it is frequently not an either/or
question.

**02. When autism and ADHD occur together, strategies can fight each other.**
They are assessed separately but often present together. The structure and predictability
that settle an autistic profile can feel like a cage to an ADHD one. It helps to ask which
need a given strategy is actually serving, rather than assuming one plan covers both.

**03. In couples, watch for the drift into a parent-child dynamic.**
One partner gradually absorbs the admin — appointments, bills, remembering things — and
the other begins to feel managed rather than partnered. Treating that as a problem with
the system the couple has fallen into, rather than a fault in either person, usually moves
further than a better chore roster.
