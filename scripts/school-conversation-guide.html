School Support Conversation Guide

A simple guide to preparing for a meeting with your child’s school. Fill in what you can; blank spaces are fine.

You don’t need the right words or a finished plan. What helps most is clear examples of what you’ve noticed and what already works. Ideas to discuss, not demands to make.

1What we are noticing, at school and at home

Focus, planning, transitions, noise, tests, homework, mornings, friendships. Real moments say more than labels.

2What tends to make things harder

Times of day, kinds of tasks, busy rooms, unexpected changes, long instructions.

3What already helps

At home, at a previous school, with a particular teacher, from a clinician. Small things count.

4Questions to ask

For the teacher, the year-level coordinator or the learning-support team.

  • What are you noticing in class, and when does it go well?
  • Which adjustments have you tried, and what happened?
  • Who is the best person to keep in touch with, and how often?
  • How will we know whether the support is working?

5Ideas to discuss

Support looks different for every student. These are starting points, not a list to insist on.

  • Clearer or shorter instructions, written down
  • Movement breaks or a job to do
  • Flexible seating
  • A quieter space for tests or focused work
  • Extra time, or work broken into steps
  • Help getting organised: diaries, checklists, reminders
  • Warning before changes to the routine
  • A regular check-in with one trusted staff member

6What to take to the meeting

  • This guide, filled in
  • Any report or letter from a clinician
  • A couple of recent examples of school work
  • Your child’s own words about what helps, if they want to share
  • Your questions, and a way to take notes

7How we will review whether support is working

Agree a date to check in, and what “working” would look like: calmer mornings, finished tasks, fewer notes home, a happier child.
